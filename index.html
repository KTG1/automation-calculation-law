The value map

From documented loss to a working estimate.

A settlement is not one number pulled from thin air. This calculator follows three connected layers so you can see where value enters—and where deductions leave.

01

Document the loss

Start with bills, missed income, damaged property, future care, and other costs you can support with records.

02

Model human impact

Use the multiplier to explore how injury severity, recovery time, and daily disruption may affect non-economic damages.

03

Account for recovery

Reduce the working value for comparative fault, legal fees, case expenses, and medical liens.

What moves a claim

The number is shaped by the story behind it.

Two claims with similar bills can resolve very differently. These are common forces behind that difference.

Evidence quality

Medical records, wage documentation, photographs, witnesses, and consistent treatment can make losses easier to establish.

Coverage available

Policy limits and the defendant’s ability to pay may cap practical recovery regardless of calculated damages.

Recovery timeline

Longer treatment, permanent limitations, and future medical needs can change both economic and non-economic damages.

Disputed responsibility

Conflicting accounts, shared fault, and local comparative-negligence rules can materially reduce or bar recovery.

Common questions

Understand the estimate.

Explore how the calculator works, what may affect a claim, and what the estimate leaves out.

How is the estimated claim value calculated?

The calculator adds economic damages to an estimate for pain and suffering. The pain-and-suffering estimate applies your selected multiplier to past and future medical expenses.

What does the likely range mean?

The range is 15% below and above the estimated take-home amount. It illustrates uncertainty and is not a prediction or guarantee.

When is the attorney fee deducted?

The calculator applies the entered attorney fee percentage after reducing the gross estimate for your share of fault.

What should I include as case costs?

Examples can include filing fees, medical records, depositions, investigators, and expert witnesses. Fee arrangements vary.

Does this calculator provide legal advice?

No. It is a general planning tool. A qualified attorney can assess deadlines, evidence, insurance coverage, local law, and the facts of a specific matter.

Can comparative fault eliminate recovery?

Rules differ by jurisdiction. Some systems reduce recovery by a percentage, while others can bar recovery at a threshold. The calculator only models a percentage reduction.
